# JustTwo

**JustTwo** is a dating and social discovery app where people meet through shared mood, activity, and real-life moments.

The core idea of the project is simple: instead of endless swiping through profiles, the user starts by choosing **how they want to meet today**.

They can search by:

* **Mood** — emotional state, vibe, intention.
* **Activity** — something they want to do with another person.
* **Today** — quick discovery for people who are open to meeting or chatting today.

The product is designed around the idea:

> **Your mood. Your plan. Just two.**

## Product Concept

JustTwo is not another generic swipe-based dating app.

Most dating apps start with photos and force users to guess each other's intentions. JustTwo starts with context.

The app asks the user:

> **How do you want to meet today?**

Then the user chooses one of the main discovery paths:

### Mood

Mood-based discovery helps users find people who are in a similar emotional state or have a similar intention.

Examples:

* Deep conversation
* Light flirting
* Calm walk
* No pressure
* Just chatting
* Ready to meet
* Slow evening talk

This mode is focused on emotional compatibility and the current state of mind.

### Activity

Activity-based discovery helps users find someone who wants to do the same thing.

Examples:

* Coffee
* Walk
* Movie
* Bar
* Voice call
* Museum
* Workout
* Weekend plan
* Dinner
* Just chat

This mode gives users a concrete reason to start a conversation.

### Today

Today mode is a fast discovery mode for people who are open to a mood or activity today.

Examples:

* Coffee today
* Walk today
* Chat tonight
* Movie today
* Meet after work
* Deep talk this evening

This mode should feel immediate, but not overly hookup-oriented. The goal is to help users create meaningful same-day moments without making the product feel cheap or purely casual.

## Core Product Difference

JustTwo is built around **intentional discovery**.

Instead of:

> Like → Match → Awkward “Hi” → Silence

JustTwo should guide users toward:

> Mood or activity → Contextual match → Natural opener → Real conversation or meeting

The app should reduce the usual friction of dating apps by giving every match a reason to exist.

## Main Product Mechanic

The main mechanic is the **Moment**.

A Moment is a temporary intent card created by the user.

A Moment can be based on mood, activity, or both.

Examples:

* “I want coffee and a calm conversation today.”
* “I’m in the mood for a deep talk.”
* “I want to go for a walk this evening.”
* “I’m open to light flirting and a drink.”
* “I want a no-pressure chat tonight.”

Moments are not permanent profile data. They describe what the user wants right now.

This makes the product feel more alive than traditional static dating profiles.

## Main Screens

### 1. Onboarding

Goal:

Explain the product quickly and emotionally.

Possible onboarding messages:

* “Meet by mood or activity.”
* “Choose how you want to connect today.”
* “Find someone for coffee, a walk, a talk, or the same vibe.”
* “Less guessing. More context.”

### 2. Home

The Home screen is the main entry point.

It should ask:

> **How do you want to meet today?**

Main cards:

* Mood
* Activity
* Today

Each card should clearly explain what happens next.

### 3. Mood Selection

The user selects their current mood or intention.

Possible mood examples:

* Deep talk
* Light flirt
* Calm walk
* No pressure
* Just chatting
* Ready to meet
* Slow evening
* Open to something real

### 4. Activity Selection

The user selects an activity.

Possible activity examples:

* Coffee
* Walk
* Movie
* Bar
* Voice call
* Dinner
* Museum
* Workout
* Weekend plan
* Just chat

### 5. Discovery Feed

The feed should show people who match the selected mood or activity.

Each profile card should show:

* Name
* Age
* Photos
* Short bio
* Current mood or activity
* Distance or approximate location, if enabled
* Intent context

Example:

> Anna
> Mood: Deep conversation
> Open to: Calm evening chat

or:

> Alex
> Activity: Coffee today
> Time: After 18:00

### 6. Profile

The user profile should include:

* Photos
* Name
* Age
* Bio
* Interests
* Preferred meeting style
* Basic dating preferences
* Safety and privacy settings

The profile should support both static identity and temporary Moments.

### 7. Matches

The Matches screen shows mutual connections.

A match should be connected to a specific context whenever possible.

Example:

> You both want coffee today.

or:

> You both chose “Deep conversation”.

This makes the first message easier.

### 8. Chat

Chat should feel clean and safe.

The app may suggest contextual openers based on the matched mood or activity.

Examples:

* “Looks like you both want coffee. Evening or weekend?”
* “You matched on deep conversation. Want to start with one honest question?”
* “You both chose a calm walk. What kind of place do you like?”

### 9. Settings

Settings should include:

* Account
* Profile editing
* Privacy
* Location preferences
* Notification settings
* Subscription settings
* Safety/reporting
* Delete account

## Privacy and Safety

Safety is important for this product.

Core safety principles:

* Users should control visibility.
* Location should be approximate, not overly precise.
* Users should be able to report and block others.
* Sensitive data should be protected.
* Private mode should allow users to be discoverable only by personal link.
* Profile sharing links should support expiration.

Possible profile link expiration options:

* 1 hour
* 24 hours
* 1 week
* 1 month
* Never expires

## AI Assistant / Cursor / Codex Context

When working on this project, always preserve the core product idea:

> JustTwo is a dating and social discovery app where users start by choosing mood or activity, then find one person for a shared moment.

Do not turn the project into a generic swipe-only dating app.

Important product rules:

* Mood and Activity are core flows, not secondary filters.
* The app should start with user intention, not with endless profile swiping.
* Moments are temporary intent cards.
* Matches should have context.
* Chat should be helped by the matched context.
* The tone should be warm, modern, human, and slightly playful.
* The product should feel safe, not cheap or overly hookup-oriented.
* UI should be clean, soft, premium, and easy to understand.
* Avoid overcomplication in the MVP.
* Prefer simple, testable architecture.
* Keep business logic separate from SwiftUI views.
* Use clear naming and avoid unnecessary abstractions.
